opinion.2 Legally (in the case of the marriage without  
manus, and only such marriages are in question here) the  
man’s property belonged to him alone and the woman’s  
to her. The fact of a marriage made no difference to 
the property of the parties; each remained sole owner  
of his or her property, could dispose of it freely and  
could be made fully liable for his or her obligations 
without restriction. There was no legal obligation  
on the woman to settle a dos on the man.3  If such a  
settlement was made the man became sole owner of 
the dowry; only in the restrictions on disposal of the imperial era,4 the legal obligation to return the dowry 
on the dissolution of the marriage, the exclusion of such  
return if the marriage was dissolved by the wife’s death and 
the husband’s right to retain certain amounts,5 are traces  
of joint rights to be found. Not till post-classical times  
do we find mention of a kind of community of goods.6
