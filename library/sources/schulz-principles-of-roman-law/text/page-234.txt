every one contains some kind of request to the addressee 
or else expressions of gratitude for some service or kindness 
rendered by his correspondent. The Roman considered 
himself bound to help his friend by no matter what sacrifice. It is significant that Cicero in all seriousness discusses the lengths to which a man might and should go in 
his fidelity to a friend.3 Laelius, into whose mouth Cicero 
puts his dialogue on friendship, militates against exaggerations and reports the case of a friend of Tiberius Gracchus 
who was said to have declared ‘ut quidquid file vellet, sibi
