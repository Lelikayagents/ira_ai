To the disinclination for general conceptions was added  
a disinclination for abstract formulation of legal rules.
In the Roman laws this disinclination sometimes led to  
great pedantry. Thus, for example, the lex Quinctia de  
aqueductibus (9 p. Chr. Bruns, No. 22) does not make use  
of a general comprehensive expression for ‘damage’ to  
aqueducts or for ‘restoration’, but prefers a tedious list  
of the different kinds of damage and restoration.
