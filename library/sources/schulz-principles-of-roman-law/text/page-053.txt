Seeing that the Roman jurists were so greatly opposed  
to the abstract it is not surprising that the question of legal 
systems interested them very little.1  2
Once more Q. Mucius was the first to attempt systematization; his ius civile  is the first systematic compilation of  
civil law.3 This work was certainly a great advance on the  
unsystematic collections of expert Opinions and Cases; it,  
however, only partially satisfies modern ideas of a system 
in which everything falls into place by classification and 
arrangement under fundamental conceptions.4 5  Still, it 
was sufficient not only for Mucius5  contemporaries but also 
in the main for the classical era. The latter, it is true, 
made the arrangement selected by Sabinus in his lihri tres 
iuris civilis  its principal civil law system, but this was only  
a rearrangement in certain particulars of Mucius5  system, 
Mucius5  framework being retained.5 The Sabinus system  
remained in the great compilations of the civil law which  
followed; when new legal conceptions had to be taken into 
consideration, the old system was enlarged by means of
