This principle is seen most clearly in the case of sale. 
A legal right to withdrawal on account of violation by 
the other party always remained foreign to Roman law; 
the right to withdrawal under §§ 325, 326 of the German 
Civil Code has its roots in German law.2 The Roman 
attitude is all the more significant as this right to withdrawal was known to Hellenistic law3 and was therefore 
often claimed by suppliants in their petitions to the 
Emperor. The imperial rescripts, however, consistently 
refused it and referred the petitioners to their right to 
fulfilment of the contract.4 As regards hire and lease, the
