NHERENT in the Roman conception of liberty are  
certain limitations; in Rome, therefore, it is possible for  
liberty to be one of the principles on which the law is based.1 
‘Freedom9 , to the Romans, never meant the capacity to  
do or leave undone what one pleased, to live at one’s own  
sweet will;2 only such were not free as were subject to a  
dominus, a master who reigned over them and in relation  
to whom they were deprived of all rights of self-determination. Thus the individual was not free when he was  
a slave, a whole nation was not free when at its head was  
an absolute monarch or when it was subject to a foreign
