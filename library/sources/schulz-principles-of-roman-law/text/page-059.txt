But if a person has inflicted a slight 
blow on a sick slave and the slave died, 
Labeo says rightly that he will be 
liable under the lex Aquilia,  because 
one thing is usually fatal to one person 
and another thing to another.
Celsus, however, says that there is a 
great difference between a case of a 
person who has ‘killed’ and the case of 
him who produced the cause from 
which death resulted: a person who has 
produced the cause of the death is not 
liable under the lex Aquilia but subject 
to an actio in factum. He gives, therefore, as an example a person who has 
given poison instead of medicine, and 
he says that this person had produced 
the cause of the death in the same way 
as a person who had given a sword to a 
madman: for this person also, he says, 
is not liable under the lex Aquilia but 
by an actio in factum.
