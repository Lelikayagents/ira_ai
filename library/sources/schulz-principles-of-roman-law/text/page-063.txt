ABSTRACTION
The above texts are disfigured by glosses and interpolations;  
the Compilers also deleted intermediate passages and abridged the 
parts retained, but that is of no consequence in the present connexion ; in a comprehensive passage such as this the systematic  
arrangement is visible as it was bound to be. Ulpian deals with the 
word 0ediere  in chapter I of the lex Aquilia.  Three sections of the 
description can be clearly distinguished: Section I determines the 
meaning of the word oeddere under the law; killing through a  
damnum corpore corpori datum 1 (7. 1-5). The second section discusses the extension of the law (by granting actiones in factum)  to 
cases of mere causam mortis praestare  (7. 6-8; 9 pr. to 3). The third  
section, finally, deals with killing caused by the combined action of 
several persons (9. 4; 11 pr. to 5); here the combined action of  
perpetrator and injured party is discussed first,2 i.e. so-called  
compensatio culpae (9. 4 and probably also 11 pr.; the beginning of 
this discussion before 9. 4 was deleted by the Compilers); then the 
common action of several perpetrators (11. 1-5). Observe first of 
all the transition from the first to the second section: the connexion 
is made by means of a careless autem. Ulpian opens the second  
section with Celsus’ formula and examples; as among these a  
case of killing by means of a supposed medicine is mentioned,  
Ulpian adds the case of a doctor who performs an operation badly 
(7. 8), although this obviously belongs under the first section. In  
the Labeo cases too there is a question of dosing with medicine  
(9 pr. § 1); when with this a case of leaving a person to starve is  
connected, the idea is: killing by handing something (poison)—and 
by not handing something (food). In the third section, after the  
discussion of compensatio culpae the following cases are dealt with: 
1. one person held the slave, another killed him (11. 1); 2. several 
persons struck together (11. 2); 3. several persons struck, but not 
simultaneously (n. 3); 4. several persons threw down a beam (11. 
4); 5. setting a dog on a person (11. 5). Here Nos. 1-3 are in order.
