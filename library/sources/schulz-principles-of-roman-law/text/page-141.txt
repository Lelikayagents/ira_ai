LIBERTY
Romans as individuals or as a people were free, however  
the degree of libertas might vary. Thus the Roman libera 
res publica existed even when Sulla was at the head of the  
State. The Roman State under the Principate was a free  
communal body,1  for the Principate was not a Dominate;1  2 
Augustus in his res gestae even boasts of having—like the  
adsertor libertatis  in a causa liberalis —reclaimed the community for freedom from the dominatio of a faction:  
(Monumentum Ancyranum 1) hem publicam domina-  
tione factionis oppressam in libertatem vindicavi’.3
Therefore the Romans designated as free even a community which, by joining the Roman alliance, was deprived of internationally recognized sovereignty; in such  
a case Roman paramountcy was not in the form of a  
Dominate. Libertas  is a truly Roman conception: clear,  
limited, practical, somewhat matter-of-fact.
