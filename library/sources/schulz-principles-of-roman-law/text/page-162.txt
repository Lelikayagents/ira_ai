It is not less characteristic of Roman freedom that both  
State and communal governments relied largely on the
initiative and public spirit of their citizens. Voluntary 
gifts played a large part in the budgets of the State and  
still more of the municipalities. Actively public-spirited,  
the Romans realized that the interest of the individual is 
inseparably bound up with that of the State.2 This  
accounts for their splendid system of public gifts, everywhere attested by inscriptions—at their head the regina
