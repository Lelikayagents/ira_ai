3. Celsus scribit, si alius mor- 
tifero vulnere percusserit, alius 
postea exanimaverit, priorem 
quidem non teneri quasi occiderit, sed quasi vulneraverit, 
quia ex alio vulnere periit; posteriorem teneri quia occidit, 
quod et Marcello videtur et est 
probabilius.
4. Si plures trabem deiecerint 
et hominem oppresserint, aeque 
veteribus placet omnes lege 
Aquilia teneri.
5. Item cum eo, qui canem 
irritaverat et effecerat, ut ali- 
quem morderet, quamvis eum 
non tenuit, Proculus respondit 
Aquliae actionem esse; sedIulia- 
anus eum demum Aquilia teneri 
ait, qui tenuit et effecit, ut ali- 
quem morderet: ceterum si non 
tenuit, in factum agendum.
Ifone person has held a slave whilst another has killed him, the former, as having furnished the cause of the death, 
will be liable to an actio in factum.
But if several persons have beaten 
a slave, we must consider whether they 
will all be liable as having killed him. 
And if it appears by whose blow he was 
killed, that man will be liable for having killed him. But if it does not 
appear, Julian says that all will be liable 
as having killed the slave, and if the 
action had been brought against one 
of them the others will not be released, [for what one person has paid 
under the lex Aquilia does not release 
the others, because it is a penalty],
Celsus writes that if one person has 
inflicted on a slave a deadly wound, 
and another has afterwards actually 
put him to death, the former will not 
be liable for having killed, but only for 
having wounded the slave, because he 
perished from another wound; but the 
latter will be liable for having killed 
the slave. Marcellus consents and it 
seems probable.
If several persons have thrown down 
a beam and thus crushed a slave, the 
ancient jurists believe that all of them 
are liable under the lex Aquilia.
Similarly Proculus has answered that 
an actio legis Aquiliae will lie against a 
person who has irritated a dog and so 
caused it to bite some one, though he 
had not hold of the dog. But Julian 
says that only the person who had hold 
of the dog and caused it to bite will be 
liable, but if he had not hold of the 
dog an actio in factum must be brought 
against him.
