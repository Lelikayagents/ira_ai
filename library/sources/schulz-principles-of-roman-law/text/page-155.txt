The rei vindicatio  could be utilized even against a person  
who had acquired the property in good faith, although it  
might be in the interest of undisturbed commerce to  
protect such person. In classical times attempts to reduce  
the harshness of the vindicatio  in these cases by means of  
a right on the part of the possessor to retain the object  
acquired in good faith until he had recovered the price  
he had paid for it were always refused.2 Significant also  
are the narrow limits set to usucafio . Acquisition of 
ownership by continued possession of a thing stolen or  
misappropriated was excluded, even when it had reached  
the hands of a possessor in good faith,3 though that is the  
principal case4 to which the institution of usucapion  
would naturally apply.
