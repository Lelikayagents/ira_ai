The style adopted in English statutes is similar. Cf, e.g., the  
Consolidation Act of 1925 (15 & 16 Geo. V, c. 49), s. 37: Tf a 
plaintiff or petitioner claims to be entitled to any equitable estate 
or right, or to relief on any equitable ground against any deed, instrument or contract, or against any right, title or claim whatsoever  
asserted by any defendant or respondent in the cause or matter, 
or to any relief founded upon a legal right, which formerly could 
only have been given by a court of equity, the court or judge shall 
give to the plaintiff or petitioner the same relief as ought formerly 
to have been given by the Court of Chancery in a suit or proceeding 
for the like purpose properly instituted.’ s. 38: Tf a defendant  
claims to be entitled to any equitable estate or right or to relief’, 
etc. The lawgiver seems unwilling to formulate the abstract rule 
that common law and equity are to be applied in the same way.
